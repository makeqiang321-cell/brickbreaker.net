What Is Arkanoid

In 1986, Taito took the brick breaker formula and added something Breakout never had: consequences for greed. Arkanoid's design team, led by Akira Fujita, introduced brick breaker power-up capsules that fall from destroyed bricks — catch one and you gain an edge. Chase one at the wrong moment and you lose a life.

The game built everything around this tension. A spaceship-shaped Vaus paddle with curved edges for precise angle control. 33 hand-designed stages with color-coded brick patterns. And at the end: DOH, a giant stone moai head that shoots back — gaming's first brick breaker boss fight.

Arkanoid sold over 200,000 arcade PCBs in Japan. It spawned sequels across every major platform. Its capsule system became the blueprint for every power-up in the genre. Breakout invented brick breaking. Arkanoid made it a real game.

3 Innovations That Changed Brick Breakers Forever

1. The Capsule Catch — Risk vs Reward

Destroy a brick. A colored capsule drops. You have half a second to decide: slide the Vaus under it, or stay put and keep the ball alive? Catch it and you're stronger. Miss it and it's gone. Miss the ball and you lose a life. No brick breaker before Arkanoid asked you to make that choice.

2. The Vaus — A Paddle With Handling

The Vaus isn't a flat bar — it's a curved spaceship-shaped energy vessel. The rounded edges produce different bounce angles than a rectangle, giving you finer control over shot direction. In Japanese arcades, you steered it with a weighted spinner knob — analog hardware precise enough to position the Vaus to the exact pixel.

3. DOH — The Boss That Shoots Back

Stage 33. The bricks clear. Then a giant Easter Island head fills the top of the screen — and it's firing energy bolts at you. DOH is constructed from brick segments you must destroy while dodging its attacks. Die during the fight and you restart the whole encounter. In 1986, a brick breaker with a boss was science fiction. Arkanoid made it real.

6 Power-Up Capsules — What Each One Does

Arkanoid's six capsules each change how you play, not just how fast you score. Know what to grab — and what to ignore.

L

Laser

Twin beams fire from the Vaus, melting through every breakable brick in one hit. Lasts until you die. The strongest offensive capsule. Always prioritize.

E

Enlarge

Vaus stretches to 1.6× width. Easier catches, but flatter bounce angles. Good for survival; bad for precision shots through narrow gaps.

C

Catch

Ball sticks to the Vaus on contact. Tap to release — aim every shot with perfect control. Turns chaos into a puzzle game.

S

Slow

Cuts ball speed in half. Your lifeline past stage 20, when the ball becomes a blur. Save it for late levels.

M

Multi-Ball

One ball → three. You only die when all balls drop. Clears bricks fast; tracking three trajectories takes practice.

1UP

Extra Vaus

Grants one extra life. Rarest capsule in the game — maybe 2–3 per full 33-stage run. Always catch this.

Grab priority: 1UP → Laser → Catch → Slow → Enlarge → Multi-Ball. Enlarge can backfire on tight layouts. Multi-Ball chaos isn't always welcome when you're cleaning up the last few bricks.

DOH — The Stage 33 Boss Fight

DOH is a giant moai statue built from brick segments, occupying the top of the screen. To beat the game, you must break every segment — while dodging the energy projectiles it fires at your Vaus.

The fight is a two-front war: aim the ball upward to chip away at DOH's body, weave the Vaus to avoid incoming shots. Laser makes it manageable. Without Laser, it's a 5-minute siege where one mistake sends you back to the start.

Proven approach: destroy DOH's outer "ear" segments first. This shrinks its hitbox and reduces projectile fire rate. Work inward toward the center. By the time you reach the core, DOH has fewer angles to shoot from — and you have fewer segments left to break.

The 1987 sequel, Revenge of Doh, brought the boss back angrier — moving mouth, faster projectiles, 34 new stages.

Arkanoid Strategy — 6 Techniques

Arcade-Tested Tactics for Reaching Stage 33

  • Laser Defines the Run. Once you have Laser, the beams clear bricks faster than the ball. Switch to defense: keep the ball alive with small Vaus moves while lasers shred rows above you. Laser + Catch together is nearly unstoppable.
  • With Catch, Aim Before You Release. Catch holds the ball on contact. Don't just tap to fire — slide the Vaus to line up the shot first, then release. Pick off isolated corner bricks surgically. This turns frantic levels into a methodical puzzle.
  • Not Every Capsule Is Worth the Risk. 1UP drops on the far left, your ball is descending on the right — you have to choose. 1UP and Laser: take the risk. Enlarge and Multi-Ball: let them go. The catch window is shorter than you think.
  • DOH: Outside-In Strategy. Target the "ears" first (outermost segments). They're the hardest to hit once the center opens up, and breaking them reduces DOH's fire rate. Save the forehead and nose for last.
  • Save Slow for Stage 20+. Ball speed escalates every few stages. By stage 20, it's punishing. Slow is your insurance policy — grab it on stage 18+ even if it means repositioning. A slow ball on stage 25 beats Laser on stage 3.
  • Snap, Don't Track. Arcade veterans flicked the spinner to snap the Vaus across the screen instantly. Same idea here: quick, decisive moves to where the ball will be, not slow tracking of where it is. Anticipate the angle, reposition, wait.

FAQ

Q: What is Arkanoid?

Arkanoid is a 1986 Taito arcade game that reinvented the brick breaker genre. It introduced 6 power-up capsules, a spaceship-shaped Vaus paddle, and DOH boss battles across 33 hand-designed stages. It sold 200,000+ arcade boards in Japan.

Q: What power-ups does Arkanoid have?

Arkanoid has 6 capsule power-ups: L (Laser) fires twin beams that melt bricks, E (Enlarge) widens the Vaus 1.6x, C (Catch) lets you hold and aim the ball, S (Slow) halves ball speed, M (Multi-Ball) splits into 3 balls, and 1UP grants an extra life.

Q: Who is the boss in Arkanoid?

DOH is the final boss at stage 33 — a giant Easter Island moai head made of brick segments. It shoots energy projectiles while you break its body. Beat it by targeting outer segments first, then working inward.

Q: How is Arkanoid different from Breakout?

Arkanoid added 6 power-up capsules, a curved Vaus paddle with spinner controller, 33 unique hand-designed stages, and DOH boss battles. Breakout had none of these — it was basic rows of bricks with flat scoring.
