Vampire Survivors, but the monsters are the 151 Pokémon of Kanto.

PokeBonk is a fan-made browser game that takes the survivors loop and drops it into Kanto. You move a starter Pokémon around a 3D arena while waves of wild Pokémon pour in from every side, and your attacks fire on their own. That means the actual game is movement and positioning — the aiming is handled for you, and the real decisions happen every time the level-up screen interrupts the chaos.

Every run starts the same way: pick Bulbasaur, Charmander, or Squirtle. Each one has a different attack and a different passive, so the choice sets the tone for the whole run. From there you kite the horde, collect the XP that drops when wild Pokémon faint, and spend your levels on upgrade cards — a new move, a damage boost, or a vitamin that nudges your stats.

The hook is evolution. Mid-run your starter evolves up its line, and each stage changes your attacks, passives, and the punch of your Ultimate. That is the moment a run turns: the move you picked two levels ago suddenly hits harder, and a build that felt weak becomes the thing that carries you through the next ten waves.

To be straight about what it is: PokeBonk is an unlicensed fan game with no official connection to Nintendo or The Pokémon Company. It uses the Pokémon names and Kanto setting as pure fan service. Progress saves locally in your browser, not to a server, so clearing your browser data wipes your League progress. But as a free, instant survivors game with all 151 Kanto Pokémon, it does the one thing it promises — and does it well.

PokeBonk Strategy — Survive the Horde

Keep moving, bank your upgrades, and save the Ultimate for when it matters.

Never stop moving. It sounds obvious, but the most common death is a player who stops to "catch their breath" right as the spawn pattern changes. Run a wide loop around the outside of the arena instead of weaving through the middle, and the horde bunches up behind you where your auto-attacks can chew through it.

Do not chase every XP gem the second it drops. Gems sit where wild Pokémon died, which is usually the middle of the crowd. Diving in for one gem puts you exactly where the next wave spawns. Let the horde thin out first, then sweep through and vacuum up the XP in one pass. The gems do not expire.

Build around the evolution. Your moves scale when you evolve, so the cards you take before that moment are the ones that matter most. If you know a Charmander run turns into Charizard mid-run, commit to moves that stay useful through that jump instead of spreading your picks thin.

Hold the Ultimate. The instinct is to pop it the first time the screen looks scary, but the Ultimate is your only "clear the board" button. Wait for the moment a wave is actually about to overrun you — you will know it, because every direction looks closed. Using it early just means it is on cooldown when you really need it.

What Makes PokeBonk Different

It is not just Vampire Survivors with a Pokémon skin.

The biggest difference is evolution. Most survivors games let you stack stats until you are an unstoppable ball of numbers, but your character is still the same person at minute one and minute twenty. In PokeBonk your kit actually changes mid-run — Charmander becomes Charmeleon becomes Charizard, and each stage swaps your attacks, passives, and Ultimate. That gives a run a shape and a turning point that the genre usually does not have.

The starter pick matters more than it looks. Because each of the three starters has a distinct attack and passive, the first choice you make is the one that echoes through the whole run. That is a real decision at second zero, where most survivors games do not give you one at all.

The fan service is the third difference. All 151 original Kanto Pokémon show up as the horde, and the fantasy is not "survive abstract blobs" — it is "survive the Kanto dex." The 3D arena with jump and slide movement also gives you two escape tools that flat survivors games simply do not have, which makes kiting feel more active.

Put it together and PokeBonk is a survivors game where the theme is not window dressing. The evolution, the starter choice, and the Kanto dex are the mechanics, not just the coat of paint — and that is what separates it from every other horde-survival clone.

PokeBonk Online — FAQ

Real answers to the questions players actually search — controls, evolution, starters, upgrade cards, whether it is official, mobile, and the game modes.

How do you play PokeBonk, and what are the controls?

PokeBonk is a survivors game: you move a starter Pokémon around a 3D Kanto arena while waves of wild Pokémon close in, and your attacks fire automatically. Move with WASD or the arrow keys, jump with Space, slide with Shift, and fire your Ultimate with R. On mobile you drag with one thumb and use on-screen buttons for the rest. Your job is not aiming — it is staying out of the middle of the horde and grabbing the XP that drops when wild Pokémon faint.

How do you evolve your Pokémon in PokeBonk?

Evolution happens automatically as your Pokémon gains levels mid-run. A Charmander evolves into Charmeleon and then Charizard, and each stage changes your attacks, passives, and the strength of your Ultimate. You do not press a button to evolve — you just keep leveling up, and the evolution triggers when you hit the right level. The practical trick is to build your upgrade cards around the evolution, because the moves you have when you evolve are the ones that scale into the stronger stage.

Which starter should you pick in PokeBonk — Bulbasaur, Charmander, or Squirtle?

Pick whichever fits how you like to kite. Charmander leans into damage and clearing waves fast, Squirtle is tankier and survives being surrounded, and Bulbasaur is the middle option with status and area coverage. None of them is a trap, because every starter can clear the game once you learn its Ultimate timing. If you are brand new, Squirtle is the most forgiving: the extra bulk buys you room to make positioning mistakes while you learn how the waves spawn.

What are the upgrade cards, and how do you build your Pokémon?

Every level-up pauses the game and offers you three upgrade cards, and you pick one. Cards give you new moves, boost your damage or attack speed, or act as vitamins that raise your stats. The build comes from stacking cards that work together — a spread of area moves to clear the horde around you, plus a single-target move for the tougher wild Pokémon, plus enough survivability to not die in one mistake. The upgrade screen is the actual game: the horde fight is just the test of whatever you picked.

Is PokeBonk an official Pokémon game, and is it free?

No, PokeBonk is an unofficial fan game made by a developer named Charcher, and it has no connection to Nintendo, Game Freak, or The Pokémon Company. It uses the Pokémon names, starters, and Kanto setting as fan service. It is completely free and runs in your browser with no download and no account, and your progress is saved locally in your browser — which also means clearing your cookies or browser data will wipe that progress.

Can you play PokeBonk on mobile?

Yes. PokeBonk runs in a mobile browser and has touch controls — drag to move and tap the on-screen buttons to jump, slide, and fire your Ultimate. Because it is a 3D WebGL game, it runs warm on older phones and the on-screen buttons are a little less precise than a keyboard, so surviving late waves is noticeably harder on a small screen. It plays best in landscape on a tablet, or on desktop with WASD.

What modes does PokeBonk have, and what is the League?

PokeBonk has Classic and Survival modes, plus clans and leaderboards. Classic is the standard run: pick a starter, survive the escalating waves, and see how far you get. Survival pushes the difficulty higher for a longer, rougher run. The League is the progression layer — clearing it unlocks new goals and gives you a longer-term reason to come back beyond a single run's score. The leaderboards rank your best runs, and clans are the social wrapper on top.
