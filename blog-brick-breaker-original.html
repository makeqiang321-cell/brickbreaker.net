Ask someone what the brick breaker original game is and you will get two answers. Older players point to Atari Breakout in 1976. Younger players remember the BlackBerry BrickBreaker that came preloaded on every device in the early 2000s. Both are right.

The story of the original brick breaker is actually two stories, and they converge on the same simple loop: paddle, ball, bricks. One version was built by two college dropouts in a garage. The other was coded by a team in Waterloo, Ontario, and shipped inside a device that executives could not put down.

What makes the brick breaker original special is not just its age. It is the fact that the formula has not changed in 50 years — and people are still playing it right now in a browser tab.

Atari Breakout

Breakout: The First Brick Breaker Built in 4 Nights

The Jobs and Wozniak Origin Story

In 1976, Atari had a problem. Its Pong clone was aging, and Nolan Bushnell wanted something fresh — a single-player game where the player broke through a wall of bricks. He handed the project to a young technician named Steve Jobs, who in turn recruited his friend Steve Wozniak to do the actual engineering.

Built in Hardware, Not Software

Wozniak built Breakout in four nights, using no CPU and no code — just hardware logic gates wired directly onto a circuit board. The core loop was already fully formed: a paddle moved left and right, a ball bounced off bricks at calculated angles, extra lives stacked up, and a score climbed at the top of the screen. It proved the formula was fun before anyone had a name for it.

Atari manufactured 11,000 Breakout cabinets and shipped them worldwide. The game was an instant hit. More importantly, the $5,000 bonus Jobs earned from the project — and the $350 he actually paid Wozniak — became one of the most infamous chapters in Silicon Valley lore.

BlackBerry

How BlackBerry BrickBreaker Reached Millions

Preloaded on Every BlackBerry Device

Starting in 1999, every BlackBerry device shipped with BrickBreaker preloaded. It had 34 levels, a trackwheel-controlled paddle, and power-ups that dropped from destroyed bricks — including lasers, multiball, and a sticky paddle that made catching the ball feel effortless. The game was not an afterthought. It was a flagship feature.

How Brick Breaker Became the Genre Name

This is the version that made "brick breaker" the generic name. People who never touched an arcade cabinet knew this game by heart. They played it during conference calls, on airplanes, and in bed with the screen brightness turned all the way down. The 34-level structure became the genre standard, copied by hundreds of clones across every platform that followed.

BlackBerry is gone now. The trackwheel is a museum piece. But the BrickBreaker it carried — the original brick breaker in the minds of an entire generation — never really went away.
